System Latency Meter

What this tool does

This tool can measure Mouse to Sound latency.

Record mouse clicks sound and speaker beeps using the microphone to measure the time until the system responds. Choose Auto to detect and average multiple attempts, or Manual to measure between two points on the sound graph.


Extra functionality: Mouse to Screen latency

This tool can be used to measure Mouse to Screen latency.

Using a high-speed camera (or a phone with slow-motion video), you can visually measure the time between the mouse moving and the screen response.

  1. Record the screen with the high-speed camera while performing the mouse click.
  2. Analyze the video frame by frame counting the frames between the mouse movement and the screen response.
  3. Calculate the latency by dividing the number of frames by the camera's frame rate.
If the video is regular speed (not slow motion), enter 1 for Slow-motion speed.
Latency (ms) = (frames ÷ saved fps) × slow-motion speed × 1000. Enter 1 for regular-speed video; enter a fraction such as 1/8 for slow motion.

How to use it
  1. Take your headset off and place its microphone as close as possible to the mouse button. The picture shows the recommended setup.
  2. Disable noise cancellation and similar audio processing in your microphone driver or software (for example, SteelSeries GG) so the microphone can clearly pick up the mouse click.
  3. Turn up your headset volume enough for the microphone to clearly pick up the beep.
  4. Press Start recording and keep quiet.
  5. Click the large test area and keep the mouse button pressed until you hear the beep, then release. Repeat 3 to 5 times for a better Auto result; Auto calculates the average latency.
  6. Press Stop recording and the sound graph will be displayed.
  7. Zoom in or out with the mouse wheel. Pan by dragging an empty area of the graph or using the scrollbar.
  1. Move the yellow M marker to the beginning of the Mouse click sound, then move the magenta B marker to the beginning of the Beep sound. The measured latency appears below the graph.
  1. The tool auto detects clicks and beeps and calculates the average latency.
  2. Make sure all clicks and beeps are correctly detected and at the beginning of the respective sounds. Adjust each event by dragging its marker or editing its table entry; remove unwanted events or add missing ones with the table controls.
Press Recalculate to run automatic detection again using the current settings. Undo and Redo cover edits.
Advanced: If the beep is too short or long, adjust its duration in Beep options.
Advanced: If the mouse-click sound is too close to the beep, add a delay in Beep options. The tool subtracts this added delay from the displayed measurement.
Advanced: If the graph is noisy, increase Zero sound below (% of peak) in Sound graph options to remove low-level microphone noise.
Auto mode sets Zero sound below (% of peak) automatically after recording to silence low-level background noise. You can adjust it to remove more noise, or lower it if quiet parts of the recording disappear.
Click threshold (% of peak) sets how loud a brief sound must be to count as a click. Raise it to ignore noise that is being mistaken for clicks; lower it if real clicks are not detected. Press Recalculate after changing it.
